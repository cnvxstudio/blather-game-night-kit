# Host checklist

## Before the session

- [ ] Pick a time and decide whether your group will meet in person or use a group call.
- [ ] Check the player limit. Blather supports 2–6 people including the host.
- [ ] Check that everyone is comfortable with English clues and typed guesses if you choose Blather.
- [ ] Open the game and understand the room-joining steps before inviting the group.
- [ ] Choose a short first session. In Blather the host can choose 1–5 rounds; choosing one round still gives everyone a turn describing.
- [ ] Have one clear invitation ready: game link, start time and where you will talk.

## While friends join

- [ ] Send the room's own invitation link, not a screenshot of an unclickable URL.
- [ ] Ask players to choose nicknames the group can recognize.
- [ ] Check that the intended players appear in the room before starting.
- [ ] Explain the central action in one sentence: one person makes clues, the others type guesses.
- [ ] In Blather, remind the describer that the secret answer stays private and that clues use the available word tiles.
- [ ] Let people ask questions before starting timed play. The preset demo is available for anyone who wants an introduction.

## During play

- [ ] Give everyone space to describe in their own way; the funniest interpretation need not be the correct one.
- [ ] Type guesses through the game's input rather than saying the answer in the call.
- [ ] In Blather, pay attention to previous guesses: the describer can quote one as a new clue.
- [ ] Let the game handle its timer and scoring. Do not invent a separate scoring rule halfway through the session.
- [ ] If somebody loses their connection, follow the options actually shown by the game. Do not assume that refreshing always restores a player's session.

## After the session

- [ ] Give people a moment to look at the result and the round's actual clues and guesses.
- [ ] If useful, fill in one row of round-log.csv. The log is a blank optional worksheet, not an automatic integration.
- [ ] Ask before sharing a player's nickname, guess or exported clip outside the group.
- [ ] Share the specific replay or question link when the product provides one. Label a general game link as a new-game invitation.
- [ ] For another game, agree who is participating before starting; people still reviewing a result may not be ready to join.
- [ ] Note one thing that made the session work, such as the group size or the chosen number of rounds, to make your next invitation easier.
