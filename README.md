# A small-group word-game night kit

A practical host checklist and a blank round log for a game night with 2–6 people. Use the planning checklist before your group meets; keep the round log private unless everyone agrees to share it.

## Start here

1. [Plan and run the session](HOST-CHECKLIST.md).
2. Download [round-log.csv](round-log.csv), or open it in a spreadsheet. The rows are blank prompts, not a record of real players or results.
3. Choose a game everyone can access. If you use Blather, the setup and play notes below describe that game specifically.

## What to arrange before people join

Decide whether you will meet in person or speak through your usual group call. Share a start time and one invitation message. Ask everyone to bring a phone, tablet or computer with a browser. Keep your group at the player limit of the game you choose.

For a first session, have a short practice explanation ready and let people ask questions before the timed play begins. Avoid explaining every scoring detail while friends are trying to join.

## Using the kit with Blather

[Blather](https://blatherround.com/) is a free English browser word party game for 2–6 players, including the host. One person knows a secret answer and builds clues from limited word tiles. The other players type guesses; a wrong guess can become part of a later clue.

- Create a room and share its invitation link.
- Friends join with nicknames, without accounts or downloads.
- Choose 1–5 rounds and take turns describing.
- Two players share a team score; groups of 3–6 have individual scores.
- After the game, look back at the actual clues and guesses before choosing anything to share.

The [one-question demo](https://blatherround.com/demo) introduces the idea before you gather a group. It is a preset introduction, not a full single-player game. Use the [current how-to-play page](https://blatherround.com/how-to-play) for the game's rules and scoring.

## Keeping a useful round log

The CSV is optional. Assign one volunteer to note a memorable clue after a round; do not make everyone stop playing to fill in a spreadsheet. Leave replay links blank when none exists. Ask before including another player's name or words in a public post.
